Brand Reflection Series · Episode 2 · companion to Color fingerprint & Sound fingerprint

Where Czech brands stand, and what's moving around them.

Homepage messaging from 23 major Czech brands embedded into a multilingual semantic space alongside 26 top rising Czech queries, projected to 2D. Dense clusters are red oceans. Isolated points are voices. Trends near empty zones are openings.

By Barbora Šandová. Source on GitHub.

Method

Brand homepage text (title, meta, headings, first paragraphs) extracted via trafilatura. Top rising Czech queries via pytrends with a hand-curated fallback when the unofficial API rate-limits. Both corpora embedded with intfloat/multilingual-e5-base (passage / query prefixes per model docs, L2-normalised). Layout via UMAP (n_neighbors=10, min_dist=0.3, cosine metric). Cluster labels are sector-rolled. Opportunity score = velocity × (1 − mean cosine-similarity to the 3 nearest brands), computed in the 768-dim embedding space, not on the 2D map.

03

Observations

01

Czech corporate-legacy brands share one homepage register.

All five banks (ČSOB, Česká spořitelna, Fio, Air Bank, Komerční) plus Generali, ČEZ and Škoda Auto cluster as one. The boundary isn't sector. It's "established Czech institution" — formal, trust-anchored, heritage-leaning. A bank's homepage and an energy utility's homepage are closer to each other than a bank's is to a fintech challenger that hasn't shipped yet.

02

The mass-market consumer cluster is a templating problem.

Nine brands across six sectors — Lidl, Albert, Rohlík (retail), McDonald's, KFC (QSR), Kofola (beverage), Pilulka (pharma), Seznam.cz (media), Avast (tech) — cluster together. They share one homepage TEMPLATE: short positioning line on top, product grid below, "akce / novinky / aktuální nabídka". The buyer who lands on any of these reads the same shape first.

03

Telecoms own a tight, isolated cluster of three.

T-Mobile, O2 and Vodafone embed close to each other and far from every other brand. The "always-on connectivity / esim / mobilní tarif" language is differentiated enough to be its own neighborhood but homogeneous enough that the three competitors are essentially interchangeable. A new entrant could occupy any direction outside this cluster.

04

Most rising trends sit in semantic territory at least one brand already occupies.

Of 26 trending queries, none find genuinely empty quadrants. The opportunity table flags about a quarter of rows as "low signal" — queries whose top three brand matches are within 0.025 cosine of each other. That's not white space; that's the embedding model telling you no brand has staked any chromatic ground in that conceptual neighborhood yet.
