Brand Reflection Series · Episode 2 · companion to Color fingerprint & Sound fingerprint

Three templates, one institution.

23 Czech brand homepages embedded in a multilingual semantic space cluster into four neighborhoods — and one of those neighborhoods contains all five banks, plus Generali, plus ČEZ, plus Škoda Auto. Sector identity we treat as obvious at the campaign level dissolves at the homepage layer. This is the map, and the moves it suggests.

By Barbora Šandová. Source on GitHub.

Method

Homepage text per brand (title, meta, first headings, first non-promo paragraphs, capped at 600 chars). Top rising Czech queries via pytrends with a hand-curated fallback. Both corpora embedded with intfloat/multilingual-e5-base (passage / query prefixes per model docs, L2-normalized). Layout via UMAP. Cluster labels rolled from sector composition. Opportunity score = velocity × (1 − mean cosine-similarity to the 3 nearest brands), computed in the 768-dim embedding space, not on the 2D map.

03

Patterns

01

One institutional brand wearing eight logos.

All five Czech banks (ČSOB, Česká spořitelna, Fio, Air Bank, Komerční), plus Generali, plus ČEZ, plus Škoda Auto land in one homepage neighborhood. Eight brands, four sectors (banking, insurance, energy, automotive), one register. The boundary lines we draw between these brands at the campaign level do not survive the homepage layer.

Why it matters"Established Czech institution" is a homepage template, not a sector. A challenger that wants to feel distinct from Česká spořitelna has to break out of the institutional template, not out of the banking sector. A bank that wants to feel different from ČEZ has the same problem.

02

The mass-market neighborhood is a homepage-shape problem.

Nine brands across six sectors — Lidl, Albert, Rohlík (retail), McDonald's, KFC (QSR), Kofola (beverage), Pilulka (pharma), Seznam.cz (media), Avast (tech) — share one homepage template: short positioning line on top, product grid below, "akce / novinky / aktuální nabídka" everywhere. Avast (security software) lands here because their homepage looks like a supermarket aisle.

Why it mattersThe buyer who lands on any of these reads the same shape first. The campaign creative might be distinctive; the homepage is not. A brand that runs distinctive campaigns onto a generic homepage trains buyers to forget the distinctive part on contact.

03

Telecoms own a tight, isolated neighborhood of three.

T-Mobile, O2 and Vodafone embed close to each other and far from every other brand. The "always-on connectivity / esim / mobilní tarif" language is differentiated enough to be its own territory, but homogeneous enough that the three competitors are essentially interchangeable inside it.

Why it mattersNo fourth brand can join the telecom neighborhood without becoming the fourth-best telecom. A new entrant could occupy any direction outside it (a telecom that reads as a lifestyle brand, a productivity brand, a cultural brand). The neighborhood boundary itself is the strategic opportunity.

04

The neighborhood boundary IS the strategic decision boundary.

The most distinctive Czech brands sit close to a neighborhood boundary, not at its center. Air Bank holds the institutional neighborhood but barely — its language tilts toward consumer. Pilsner Urquell holds the food & beverage neighborhood but tilts toward heritage / luxury. Strong brands are edge brands; safe brands are centroid brands.

Why it matters"Which competitor do we want to look like" is the wrong question. The right question is "which homepage neighborhood do we want to leave." Most brand strategies adjust within the neighborhood (Air Bank vs Komerční); the bigger move is between neighborhoods (Air Bank vs Avast vs Albert).

04

Playbook

Five moves a strategist or creative director can run on next week's pitch using this map.

01

Score the client's homepage before writing the brief.

Run the client URL through the same pipeline as Episode 2. If they're in the mass-market neighborhood, every campaign starts with the same handicap: the homepage trains buyers to expect a promo template. If they're in the institutional neighborhood, campaign creative has to actively pull buyers out of "Czech legacy institution" framing.

02

Identify your homepage's defining vocabulary, then refuse to use it.

Each homepage neighborhood has a TF-IDF token signature ("akce / novinky / magazín / leták" for mass-market; "bankou / silnější / partnerem" for institutional). A brief that wants to escape its neighborhood lists those tokens explicitly and rules them out. Escape is a word-level constraint, not just a creative ambition.

03

Position against neighborhood boundaries, not against named competitors.

A pitch slide showing "we differ from Komerční banka because X" is intra-neighborhood wallpaper change. A pitch slide showing "we will move from the institutional neighborhood toward the consumer one — here's the homepage shape we'll adopt" is a real positioning move. Re-score after the rebrand. If the homepage didn't move neighborhoods, neither did the brand.

04

Use Brief Generator for category-specific numerical targets.

The map tells you which neighborhood the client sits in. The Spot Scorer's Brief Generator tells you the numerical targets a distinctive spot in that sector needs to hit. Topography identifies the trap; the brief generator gives the spec for breaking out of it.

05

Read trends through the neighborhood lens, not the brand lens.

A rising query that lands in an empty zone of the map is a real opening for the brand whose neighborhood boundary touches that zone. A rising query that lands inside a dense neighborhood is an opportunity only for the brand willing to break its homepage vocabulary. Saturation score in the opportunity table is the diagnostic.
