Three templates, one institution.

23 Czech brand homepages embedded in a multilingual semantic space cluster into four neighborhoods — and one of those neighborhoods contains all five banks, plus Generali, plus ČEZ, plus Škoda Auto. Sector identity we treat as obvious at the campaign level dissolves at the homepage layer. This is the map, and the moves it suggests.

Data & cloud engineer at Alma Career. Built across a few weekends to test what brand competitive analysis looks like when it starts from public data instead of from pitch decks. Part of a five-piece Brand Reflection Series.

By Barbora Šandová. Source on GitHub.

Eight brands. Four sectors. One homepage template.

ČSOB, Česká spořitelna, Fio banka, Air Bank, Komerční banka, Generali, ČEZ, Škoda Auto. The boundary between them, on the homepage layer, is zero.

Method

Homepage text per brand (title, meta, first headings, first non-promo paragraphs, capped at 600 chars). Top rising Czech queries via pytrends with a hand-curated fallback. Both corpora embedded with intfloat/multilingual-e5-base (passage / query prefixes per model docs, L2-normalized). Layout via UMAP. Cluster labels rolled from sector composition. Opportunity score = velocity × (1 − mean cosine-similarity to the 3 nearest brands), computed in the 768-dim embedding space, not on the 2D map.

02

One brand, end to end: Air Bank

If the map is the diagnostic, this is what doing something with it looks like. The brand is Air Bank; the moves stay the same for any brand on the map. Four steps from "where am I" to "what slides go in the deck."

01

Where Air Bank sits on the map

Air Bank embeds inside the Banking + heavy industry neighborhood, next to ČSOB, Česká spořitelna, Fio banka, Komerční banka, Generali, ČEZ, and Škoda Auto. Three banks plus an insurer plus an energy company plus a carmaker all read as one brand to the homepage layer.

On the map, Air Bank tilts slightly toward the consumer side of the institutional neighborhood — closer to the boundary than its peers. That tilt is the only visible difference between Air Bank and Komerční, and it's small.

02

The vocabulary it shares with seven other brands

The defining tokens for the institutional neighborhood, derived directly from homepage TF‑IDF, are: banka, škoda, čez, fio, česká. These are the words that distinguish this neighborhood from the other three on the map.

If Air Bank's homepage shares those tokens with ČEZ's homepage, then to a buyer's first read, Air Bank is ČEZ. The challenger positioning — "the bank that's different from Komerční" — is decided in the campaign layer and quietly undone in the homepage layer.

03

Which playbook moves apply, in order

Move 02 — list the institutional vocabulary explicitly in the brief and rule it out. Not as creative aspiration; as a word-level constraint. The first draft that lands with banka, partnerem or silnejší on the homepage goes back.

Move 03 — stop pitching against named banks. The boundary that matters is the one between the institutional neighborhood and the mass-market consumer neighborhood. The target isn't the center of mass-market (Lidl, Albert) — it's the edge closest to Air Bank's current position, held by brands like Pilulka and Avast that read as consumer without reading as supermarket. That edge is the strategic ask.

Move 01 — after the rebrand, re-score. If the homepage didn't move neighborhoods, neither did the brand. Soft success at the campaign layer with no movement at the homepage layer is the failure mode this episode is built to catch.

03

Generate the same brief for any brand

The Air Bank brief above is one application of the diagnostic; this generator runs the same algorithm for any brand in the dataset. Token lists come from the TF‑IDF signature of each cluster; trajectory candidates come from each brand's nearest non-current cluster centroid and the brands in it ranked by 2D distance.

The generator is mechanical. Geometrically nearest does not mean strategically right. Pilsner Urquell's nearest neighborhood is mass-market consumer, but recommending a premium beer drift toward Lidl is obviously wrong. Use the output as a structured starting point for a strategist's judgment, not as a directive. The Air Bank brief above shows what the human edit looks like.

Four patterns the map reveals

Each pattern is a finding that the map made visible. Patterns explain why the four neighborhoods matter; the Air Bank walk-through shows how to act on them.

01

One institutional brand wearing eight logos.

All five Czech banks (ČSOB, Česká spořitelna, Fio, Air Bank, Komerční), plus Generali, plus ČEZ, plus Škoda Auto land in one homepage neighborhood. Eight brands, four sectors (banking, insurance, energy, automotive), one register. The boundary lines we draw between these brands at the campaign level do not survive the homepage layer.

Why it matters"Established Czech institution" is a homepage template, not a sector. A challenger that wants to feel distinct from Česká spořitelna has to break out of the institutional template, not out of the banking sector. A bank that wants to feel different from ČEZ has the same problem.

02

The mass-market neighborhood is a homepage-shape problem.

Nine brands across six sectors — Lidl, Albert, Rohlík (retail), McDonald's, KFC (QSR), Kofola (beverage), Pilulka (pharma), Seznam.cz (media), Avast (tech) — share one homepage template: short positioning line on top, product grid below, "akce / novinky / aktuální nabídka" everywhere. Avast (security software) lands here because their homepage looks like a supermarket aisle.

Why it mattersThe buyer who lands on any of these reads the same shape first. The campaign creative might be distinctive; the homepage is not. A brand that runs distinctive campaigns onto a generic homepage trains buyers to forget the distinctive part on contact.

03

Telecoms own a tight, isolated neighborhood of three.

T-Mobile, O2 and Vodafone embed close to each other and far from every other brand. The "always-on connectivity / esim / mobilní tarif" language is differentiated enough to be its own territory, but homogeneous enough that the three competitors are essentially interchangeable inside it.

Why it mattersNo fourth brand can join the telecom neighborhood without becoming the fourth-best telecom. A new entrant could occupy any direction outside it (a telecom that reads as a lifestyle brand, a productivity brand, a cultural brand). The neighborhood boundary itself is the strategic opportunity.

04

The neighborhood boundary IS the strategic decision boundary.

The most distinctive Czech brands sit close to a neighborhood boundary, not at its center. Air Bank holds the institutional neighborhood but barely — its language tilts toward consumer. Pilsner Urquell holds the food & beverage neighborhood but tilts toward heritage / luxury. Strong brands are edge brands; safe brands are centroid brands.

Why it matters"Which competitor do we want to look like" is the wrong question. The right question is "which homepage neighborhood do we want to leave." Most brand strategies adjust within the neighborhood (Air Bank vs Komerční); the bigger move is between neighborhoods (Air Bank vs Avast vs Albert).

Full playbook (five moves)

Five moves a strategist or creative director can run on next week's pitch. Moves 01–03 already show up in the Air Bank walk-through; 04 and 05 cover trends and re-scoring.

01

Score the client's homepage before writing the brief.

Run the client URL through the same pipeline as Episode 2. If they're in the mass-market neighborhood, every campaign starts with the same handicap: the homepage trains buyers to expect a promo template. If they're in the institutional neighborhood, campaign creative has to actively pull buyers out of "Czech legacy institution" framing.

02

Identify your homepage's defining vocabulary, then refuse to use it.

Each homepage neighborhood has a TF-IDF token signature ("akce / novinky / magazín / leták" for mass-market; "bankou / silnější / partnerem" for institutional). A brief that wants to escape its neighborhood lists those tokens explicitly and rules them out. Escape is a word-level constraint, not just a creative ambition.

03

Position against neighborhood boundaries, not against named competitors.

A pitch slide showing "we differ from Komerční banka because X" is intra-neighborhood wallpaper change. A pitch slide showing "we will move from the institutional neighborhood toward the consumer one — here's the homepage shape we'll adopt" is a real positioning move. Re-score after the rebrand. If the homepage didn't move neighborhoods, neither did the brand.

04

Use Brief Generator for category-specific numerical targets.

The map tells you which neighborhood the client sits in. The Spot Scorer's Brief Generator tells you the numerical targets a distinctive spot in that sector needs to hit. Topography identifies the trap; the brief generator gives the spec for breaking out of it.

05

Read trends through the neighborhood lens, not the brand lens.

A rising query that lands in an empty zone of the map is a real opening for the brand whose neighborhood boundary touches that zone. A rising query that lands inside a dense neighborhood is an opportunity only for the brand willing to break its homepage vocabulary. Saturation score in the opportunity table is the diagnostic.

What this is and isn't (limits + robustness)

Honest disclosure. The report is exploratory analysis on a small hand-curated sample, not peer-reviewed methodology. Strategists who plan to act on it should know what holds and what doesn't.

  • Sample is 24 brands, hand-picked. Findings condition on this set. Claims about "Czech advertising overall" would need a wider corpus. The Brand Reflection Series uses the same 24 brands across all episodes so cross-episode comparisons are internally consistent; external generalization is a separate question.
  • k=4 clusters chosen by inspection, not by silhouette or gap statistic. Qualitative findings (banks cluster tightly; telecoms occupy their own tight neighborhood; mass-market and institutional are clearly distinct) hold across reasonable choices of k. Specific counts ("eight brands in one neighborhood") are sensitive to k and to UMAP random seed.
  • TF-IDF tokens include brand names by construction. "banka, škoda, čez, fio" appear as top tokens for the institutional cluster partly because those brands are in it. A stricter analysis would strip proper nouns and re-derive. The strategic interpretation (a shared institutional register) survives; the exact token list is partly self-referential.
  • Homepage text is a narrow proxy for brand identity. ~600 characters per brand. Brands can position differently in TV, OOH or social and still get classified by this homepage snippet. The map describes "how the homepage reads," not "how the brand is perceived overall." Treat homepage as one signal among many.
  • Exploratory, not inferential. No formal test for the headline claim. The map is descriptive evidence consistent with "sector identity dissolves at the homepage layer," not a rejection of a pre-specified null hypothesis. The right framing is a lens that surfaces patterns worth investigating, not a causal conclusion.
  • The Air Bank brief is a recommendation, not a tested prescription. No counterfactual evidence that drifting toward the mass-market edge causes the perception shift. The brief is a structured starting point for a strategist, not an experimentally validated playbook. The success metric (re-embed after 90 days, observe drift) is the in-flight validation, not a pre-validation.
